“Moving to the Left” (from “[Shift-left testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shift-left_testing)”) is a principle in which we try to move feedback in a process as early as possible, as it decreases the cost of changing or fixing something in case it is or goes wrong.

Many of the projects I try to get involved in come from this need (and desire!) to move stuff left, and a lot of the practices and tools I use and recommend help with that.

- 📅 Are you scheduling a meeting? Move left by providing an agenda so people can come prepared, involve the required people, or decline to attend.
- 🤝 Are you hiring? Move left by defining what you need from them, and how you are going to define if you extend an offer or not
- 🏗️ Are you planning on building something new? Move left by writing an Request for Comments, so both stakeholders and experts can evaluate and give advice
- ⌨️ Are you writing something (code, documents, etc.)? Move left by having your tools give you immediate feedback (things like standard configurations for IDEs, pre-commit hooks, formatters, etc.)
